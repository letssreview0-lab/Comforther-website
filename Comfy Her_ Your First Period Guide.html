What is a period?

It’s a normal, healthy part of growing up. Here’s the simple version.

1
Your body is growing up.
During puberty, hormones (your body’s chemical messengers) start changing your body.
2
Each month it gets ready.
The lining of the uterus (the womb) builds up softly, just in case it’s ever needed one day.
3
Then it lets go.
When the lining isn’t needed, it leaves your body through the vagina as a little blood. That’s your period. It’s not dirty or shameful. It’s your body working as it should.

What to expect

Every girl is different, so don’t compare. These are common patterns.

10–15the age most girls get their first period
3–7days a period usually lasts
21–45days between periods is common in teens
🎨

Colour

Period blood can look brown, pink, or dark red. All of these can be normal, especially at the start.

🌀

Irregular at first

Early periods are often unpredictable. You might skip a month. It can take a few years for your cycle to settle.

🌤️

Feelings

You might feel tired, bloated, moody, or get cramps or pimples. These are common and usually pass.

Pads, liners & more

There are lots of choices. Pick what feels comfortable, and ask a trusted adult if you’re unsure.

🩷

Pads

The easiest place to start. They stick inside your underwear. Change every 4 to 6 hours, or sooner if it feels full.

🩲

Period underwear

Washable, reusable underwear with a built-in absorbent layer. Great for light days or as a backup.

🧷

Tampons & cups

Some girls try these later, when they feel ready. There’s no rush. If you use a tampon, change it at least every 8 hours.

Wash your hands before and after changing. Wrap used pads, put them in a bin, and never flush them.

Feeling better on period days

♨️

Warmth

A warm (not hot) water bottle or heating pad on your tummy can ease cramps.

🚶‍♀️

Gentle movement

A slow walk or light stretching can help some girls feel better.

💧

Water & food

Drink water and eat regular, nourishing meals. Fruit, veg, and iron-rich foods like beans, lentils, and leafy greens are great.

😴

Rest

It’s okay to slow down. Extra sleep and quiet time are a good thing.

If cramps hurt a lot, talk to a parent or doctor about what pain relief is safe for you. Always follow the label and their advice.

Myths & facts

Myth: Periods are dirty or shameful.

Fact: A period is a natural, healthy process. Being clean means washing and changing regularly, and that’s all.

Myth: You can’t exercise, bathe, or wash your hair on your period.

Fact: You can do all of these. Bathing and washing your hair are healthy any day of the month.

Myth: Everyone can tell when you’re on your period.

Fact: Nobody can tell. With a fresh pad and a spare in your bag, you can go about your day as normal.

Myth: Something is wrong if my period is late or skips a month.

Fact: Irregular periods are very common in the first couple of years. See the section below for when to check in with a doctor.

Talk to someone you trust

You never have to figure this out alone. A mum, aunty, sister, teacher, school nurse, or doctor can help.

It’s a good idea to see a doctor if:
  • You’re 15 and haven’t had a period yet
  • Periods come more often than every 21 days or less often than every 45 days
  • Bleeding lasts more than 7 days, or you soak through a pad in an hour or two
  • Cramps are so strong they stop you from school or daily life
  • You feel unwell, dizzy, or very tired a lot, or you have a fever (especially while using a tampon)
